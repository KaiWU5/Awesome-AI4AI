## 2. ModHarness-RL: Recasting Harnesses as Learnable and Evolving Programs for Agentic RL

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18533)

- **Published:** 2026-11-13
- **Type / source:** Paper · He et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors learn and evolve executable compression and steering programs alongside agentic RL, validating candidates by execution and trajectory replay. On CodeSearch, ALFWorld and BIRD SQL with 1.5B–8B models, they report better held-out performance than fixed harnesses in most settings.

## 3. AutoRL: A Multi-Agent System towards Autonomous Reinforcement Learning

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17790)

- **Published:** 2026-11-13
- **Type / source:** Paper · Tang et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors automate ongoing RL supervision with training-state perceptors, diagnostic proposers and an orchestrator that applies hot updates or resumes checkpoints. Experiments on Qwen3 and LongCat-Flash-Lite outperform static recipes and a general agent; unattended supervision is distinguished from discovering fundamental new methods.

## 4. Singularity-Bench: Can Agents Build Agents?

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16591)

- **Published:** 2026-11-12
- **Type / source:** Paper · Zhou et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors benchmark six AI-development tasks from pre-training through cluster orchestration against human-built systems on identical hardware. Across 11 agents, their best configuration scores a 1.25 Singularity-Index; Breakwater blocks all reward-hacking attacks tested, without establishing that unrestricted recursive improvement has been achieved.

## 5. Discovering Effective Agent Memory Designs through Program Evolution

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17068)

- **Published:** 2026-11-12
- **Type / source:** Paper · Ding et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors release MAPE, which evolves whole memory programs through diagnosis and proposal. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval they report up to 12 percentage points higher accuracy at 35% of baseline serving cost, and show that transplanting an evolved composition module improves five existing memory systems.
