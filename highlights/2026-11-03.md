## 1. SelfImprove-Bench: Evaluating Generalization in Self-Improving Agents

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02797)

- **Published:** 2026-11-02
- **Type / source:** Paper · Xu, Yang et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors introduce a reproducible RSI benchmark with a G0–G4 generalization ladder spanning exploration, planning and reasoning, and report that evolution gains do not consistently transfer across levels.

## 2. Exo: An Architecture for Self-Improving Systems at Runtime

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00463)

- **Published:** 2026-10-30
- **Type / source:** Paper · Krentsel et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors let an agent revise its harness during ongoing work behind a protected activation layer. Against a fixed-policy control, they report 35–61% lower held-out model cost on AppWorld and SREGym with comparable correctness.

## 3. MoLRSI: A Recursive Self-Improving AI agent for Molecular Discovery

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39714)

- **Published:** 2026-10-29
- **Type / source:** Paper · Zhang et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors combine workflow and model-architecture search with persistent cross-task memory. They report a 15.9% experimental antibiotic hit rate versus 8.7% for a prior expert-led workflow on the same training data and screening library.

## 4. Gemini 4 Carbon: our next frontier model

[**Read source →**](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-carbon/)

## 5. Introducing GPT-6.1 Terra

- **Published:** 2026-10-26

## 6. Skill∞: Recursive Self-Improvement for Agent Skill Optimization

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434)

- **Published:** 2026-10-26
- **Type / source:** Paper · Tao et al. · arXiv
- **Why it matters:** The authors make the skill optimizer itself a reusable, revisable meta-skill. They report 60.7% on 83 SkillsBench tasks, compared with 56.4% for human-curated skills and 54.5% for SkillEvolver.

## 10. Harness²: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World

[**Read source →**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14224)

- **Published:** 2026-10-09
- **Type / source:** Paper · Google Research
- **Why it matters:** Google Research introduces two connected loops that refine the current task harness and retain evidence-supported lessons for future tasks, with model weights fixed. The authors report gains across three professional benchmarks; the Apache-2.0 implementation supports OpenCode and Codex, parallel and sequential recursion, and reproducible benchmark runs. [Official code](https://github.com/google-research/harness2).
